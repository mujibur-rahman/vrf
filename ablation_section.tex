\subsection{Ablation Study}

We isolate the contribution of the two components under 20\% adversarial
clients on NYC-Taxi (mean over 5 seeds). The two factors are the
\emph{selection} mechanism and the \emph{aggregation} rule. For the selection
axis we contrast a biasing coordinator that concentrates the committee to an
adversarial share $\beta=0.5$ (``no VRF'') against VRF self-selection, which by
Proposition~\ref{prop:concentration} holds $\beta$ at the population rate
$\alpha=0.2$; for the aggregation axis we contrast the sample mean (FedAvg)
against the proposed trust-weighted rule. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the
four variants.

The two components are \emph{necessary and complementary} rather than additively
incremental. Without VRF, the coordinator concentrates malicious clients in the
committee and prediction error diverges by three-to-five orders of magnitude for
\emph{both} the mean and the trust-weighted rule (FedAvg and ``Trust only'') --
trust-aware aggregation cannot recover once the committee is dominated. Without
trust, mean aggregation still diverges under model poisoning even at the
VRF-bounded $\beta=0.2$ (``VRF only'', MAE $225.6$), because a norm-inflated
minority overwhelms the average; under collusion, where magnitudes are milder,
VRF-bounded mean aggregation is already stable ($0.268$) and trust further lowers
error and malicious influence ($0.252$, influence $0.033\!\to\!0.024$). Only the
full method is robust under both attacks (MAE $0.238$ / $0.252$). This confirms
the design rationale: VRF-based selection bounds adversarial concentration during
participation, and trust-aware aggregation limits the impact of the remaining
malicious updates -- and neither alone suffices.
